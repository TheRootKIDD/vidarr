# bigmoe — Reading list (Phase-0 literature refresh)

First compiled from memory on 2026-09-02; **verified the same day** against arXiv / proceedings / vendor documents, entry by entry, with work through mid-2026 added. Format: `Authors year — Title (id). Quantitative takeaway. → H`. Every entry carries a number, not an adjective; where a paper sets a prior for one of ADR-013's thresholds it appears again in §Priors. Entries that were wrong, vague or unconfirmable are in §Flagged. The author's in-depth document (the lnkd.in link at the end of the note → a public Google Doc, *Designing AI Chip Hardware and Software*, 2026, 154 pp.) is fetched to `docs/source/` and summarised against P1–P19 in `docs/07-author-hardware-doc.md`.

**Headline priors against the note's claims** (details in §Priors; thresholds stay as ADR-013 set them — they are the note's claims, the priors say which are expected to fail at our scale):
- **H4** (coarse experts, $g$ = 1): the granularity scaling law's fit predicts ≈ 0.14 nats (≈ 3 %) worse than $g$ = 4 at `small` — expected to *fail* the 1 % threshold; the question is by how much and whether L7b ($L_e$ = 2) recovers it.
- **H3** (2:4 at 2× width): 2:4 *at equal width* from scratch costs +1.5 % (124 M) to +3.8 % (1.5 B); no precedent for the 2×-width claim; end-to-end speedups 1.2× on Ampere — consistent with I15's 1.4× pricing. Activation 2:4 is free; FP4 training sits at 0.8–1.5 % gap, FP8 < 0.25 %.
- **H13** (4 MTP heads, no main-loss regression): heads 2–4 acceptance 75–90 % at ≥ 7 B with sequential modules, but MTP *hurts* main quality below ≈ 1 B; a forward curriculum is the documented fix. Expect a regression at 80 M.
- **H6** (shared block vs stacked MoE): both positive (MoEUT, tied experts, Mixture-of-Recursions) and negative (iso-depth looping law, ALBERT) priors; shared-expert effects < 0.001 nats.
- **H7** (≥ 30 % FLOPs at ≤ 1 %): at or below what MoD, MoR, Ouro and CALM report — plausible.
- **H15a** (≤ 2 %): YOCO matches the scaling curve 160 M–13 B with one global cache from one representation per token; losing far context entirely costs ≈ 2.4 %; no measured gain from gradient through memory (ADR-023 stop is safe).
- **H15b** (≤ 1 % long-context): *below* the only ≈ 100 M prior (Landmark, head-shared block selection, +1.5 % ppl at 2 k); large trained block-sparse (NSA, MoBA, DSA) is within ± 0.5 pt at ≥ 500 M. IVF on attention queries needs 30–50 % of lists scanned (RetrievalAttention): `007-faiss-1m` recall must be re-measured on real block keys before H15b-S is scored (I14).
- **H9** (≥ 3× upper-tier bytes): ≈ 3.8× from placement alone, ≈ 1.9× from multicast alone in the literature — plausible combined. **H5** (≤ 5 %): tighter than the one measured rail-only overhead (8–11 %).
- **H10**: EP8 → EP32 gives 1.8×, so the 10 % pooling threshold is easy. **H14**: supported in decode-heavy regimes, loses under tight TPOT — S5 must sweep the SLO pair.
- **H17**: memory-layer gains start at ≈ 1 M keys (Memory+, Engram +3.7 ARC-C iso-FLOPs at 27 B), matching our 2²⁰–2²¹ table. **H18**: rank ≤ 4 holds for post-training-size data, weakens for large-data adaptation; multi-tenant overhead 5–20 % at 10³ adapters, so H18-S's 5 % is optimistic.
- **Memory prices (P16/P19)**: 2026 is a shortage year (DDR5 +480 % YoY, enterprise SSD 6.5×); scenario files carry a trough column.

## P2 — Parallel attention + FF
- Wang & Komatsuzaki 2021 — GPT-J-6B (github.com/kingoflolz/mesh-transformer-jax; code release, no paper). Origin of the parallel block (one LN, attention and FF branches summed) at 6 B, motivated by saving one TP all-reduce per layer; no ablation published. → H2
- Chowdhery et al. 2022 — PaLM: Scaling Language Modeling with Pathways (arXiv:2204.02311). Parallel layers ≈ 15 % faster training at 540 B (fused attention/MLP input matmuls); ablation: small quality loss at 8 B, none at 62 B. → H2
- Almazrouei et al. 2023 — The Falcon Series of Open Language Models (arXiv:2311.16867). Parallel attention + MLP at 7/40/180 B: "no measurable degradation" in zero-shot or perplexity, all-reduces per layer 2 → 1; MQA ablation at 1 B/3 B: −0.7/−1.7 zero-shot points. → H2
- *Added:* Sonkar & Baraniuk 2023 — Investigating the Role of FFNs in Transformers Using Parallel Attention and FF Net Design (arXiv:2305.13297). Encoder-only PAF vs SAF: GLUE 92.2 vs 92.8 (RoBERTa-large, PAF on 10× less data), 89.5 vs 89.6 (BERT-large); no speed numbers. → H2
- *Added:* Cohere 2025 — Command A: An Enterprise-Ready Large Language Model (arXiv:2504.00698). 111 B production model uses parallel transformer blocks; report claims equivalent quality with a "significant" throughput gain but publishes no number. → H2
- *Added:* Apple 2025 — Apple Intelligence Foundation Language Models: Tech Report 2025 (arXiv:2507.13575). Server model is a Parallel-Track MoE (PT-MoE: parallel tracks + interleaved global/local attention); no quality or latency figures in the report. → H2
- *Added:* Kim et al. 2025 — First Attentions Last: Better Exploiting First Attentions for Efficient Transformer Training (arXiv:2510.14614). Feeds the first MHA output to later MLPs, removing per-block MHA→MLP dependency: multi-GPU training time −44 %, single-GPU throughput up to 1.18×, perplexity ≤ GPT baseline. → H2
- *Added:* Wang et al. 2026 — Parallel Track Transformers: Enabling Fast GPU Inference with Reduced Synchronization (arXiv:2602.07306). Apple PT design: up to 16× fewer TP sync ops; TTFT −15–30 %, TPOT −2–12 %, throughput up to +31.9 % in TensorRT-LLM/vLLM at "competitive" quality (no loss number). → H2 (systems half)

## P3 — Sparsity and low precision
- Mishra et al. 2021 — Accelerating Sparse Deep Neural Networks (arXiv:2104.08378). Defines 2:4 Sparse Tensor Cores (2× matmul throughput) and the dense-train → prune → retrain recipe that recovers accuracy across vision/NLP; not from-scratch training. → H3
- Frankle & Carbin 2019 — The Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (arXiv:1803.03635, ICLR 2019). Unstructured subnets at 10–20 % of weights match dense accuracy when reset to original init; found by iterative prune-retrain. You et al. 2020 — Drawing Early-Bird Tickets (arXiv:1909.11957, ICLR 2020): the mask emerges in the first epochs at large LR; up to 4.7× training-energy saving. Both vision, unstructured. → H3 (fixed-mask-early premise)
- Evci et al. 2020 — Rigging the Lottery: Making All Tickets Winners (arXiv:1911.11134, ICML 2020). Dynamic sparse training at fixed parameter count (drop by magnitude, grow by gradient) approaches dense ResNet-50 accuracy at 80–90 % unstructured sparsity given up to 5× longer training. → H3
- NVIDIA (Abecassis, Agrusa et al.) 2025 — Pretraining Large Language Models with NVFP4 (arXiv:2509.25149). 12 B hybrid Mamba-Transformer, 10 T tokens, all GEMMs NVFP4 (RHT, 2-D scaling, stochastic rounding, some BF16 layers): loss within 1 % of FP8 in the stable phase, ~1.5 % in decay, 0.5 % after BF16 forward for the last 18 %; MMLU-Pro 62.58 vs 62.62. → H3 (FP4)
- DeepSeek-AI 2024 — DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report (arXiv:2412.19437). FP8 E4M3 with tile/block scaling and high-precision accumulation: relative loss error < 0.25 % vs BF16 on V2-Lite/V2-scale runs (~1 T tokens); used for the 671 B / 14.8 T run. → H3 (FP8 floor)
- *Added:* Hu et al. 2024 — Accelerating Transformer Pre-training with 2:4 Sparsity (arXiv:2404.01847, ICML 2024). 2:4 FFN weights at equal width from scratch: GPT-2 val loss +1.5 % (124 M), +2.7 % (350 M), +2.8 % (774 M), +3.8 % (1.5 B) vs dense; FFN ≤ 1.7×, block ≈ 1.3×, end-to-end 1.18–1.21× on RTX 3090. → H3
- *Added:* Haziza et al. 2025 — Accelerating Transformer Inference and Training with 2:4 Activation Sparsity (arXiv:2503.16672). 2:4 on squared-ReLU activations, weights dense: 1.5 B / 63 B tokens ppl 2.652 vs 2.651 dense; FFN fwd+bwd up to 1.3× on H100; no end-to-end training speedup measured. → H3 (activation, not weight, sparsity)
- *Added:* Madhyastha et al. 2026 — To 2:4 Sparsity and Beyond: Neuron-level Activation Function to Accelerate LLM Pre-Training (arXiv:2602.06183). 2:4 weights plus v:n:m activations on all FFN matmuls, dense steps at the end: 1.4–1.7× end-to-end pretraining on A100+ at matched benchmark quality. → H3
- *Added:* Neural Magic / Red Hat 2025 — Sparse-Llama-3.1-8B-2of4 (HF model card + Red Hat Developer post, Feb 2025; no arXiv). 2:4 on all linear layers + 13 B-token distillation: 98.4 % OpenLLM-v1 recovery (62.16 vs 63.19); vLLM +30 % throughput / −20 % latency from sparsity alone on Ampere+. → H3 (post-training, inference)
- *Added:* Tseng et al. 2025 — Training LLMs with MXFP4 (arXiv:2502.20586, AISTATS 2025). Stochastic rounding + random Hadamard transform: GPT up to 6.7 B with > ½ of FLOPs in MXFP4, "near-lossless" vs BF16 (no number in abstract); backward > 1.3× FP8, > 1.7× BF16. → H3 (FP4)
- *Added:* Castro et al. 2025 — Quartet: Native FP4 Training Can Be Optimal for Large Language Models (arXiv:2505.14669). FP4 scaling law on 30 M–7 B Llama: MXFP4 parameter efficiency 0.65, data efficiency 0.95 vs BF16; Blackwell kernels ≈ 1.6× FP8 end-to-end (fwd ≤ 2×, bwd ≤ 1.5×). → H3 (FP4)
- *Added:* Mishra et al. 2025 — Recipes for Pre-training LLMs with MXFP8 (arXiv:2506.08027). MXFP8-E4M3 with a specific conversion rule matches BF16 training up to 8 B / 15 T tokens on Blackwell. → H3 (FP8 floor)
- *Added:* Panferov et al. 2026 — Quartet II: Accurate LLM Pre-Training in NVFP4 by Improved Unbiased Gradient Estimation (arXiv:2601.22813). MS-EDEN unbiased quantiser: > 2× lower quantisation error than stochastic rounding; fully NVFP4 linear layers up to 1.9 B / 38 B tokens; up to 4.2× over BF16 on Blackwell. → H3 (FP4)
- *Added:* Cim et al. 2026 — Pretraining Large Language Models with MXFP4 on Native FP4 Hardware (arXiv:2605.09825). Llama-3.1-8B on C4, AMD MI355X: quantising Wgrad is the main convergence cost; FP4 in Fprop+Dgrad alone costs only "modest" extra tokens. → H3 (supports weight-only fake-quant as the mild case)
- *Added:* Ding et al. 2026 — Full-Stack FP4: Stable LLM Pretraining with Quantized Projections, Optimizers, and Attention (arXiv:2607.04422). 3 B / 64 B tokens with FP4 projections, optimiser states and attention: loss 2.286 vs 2.267 BF16 (+0.84 %), zero-shot ppl equal; RTX 5090 2.5–2.8× on optimiser compute. → H3 (FP4)

## P4 / P8 — Expert granularity
- Krajewski et al. 2024 (ICML 2024; proceedings list Ludziejewski first, equal contribution) — Scaling Laws for Fine-Grained Mixture of Experts (arXiv:2402.07871). Fit $L = c + (g/G^{\gamma} + a)N^{-\alpha} + bD^{-\beta}$, $\gamma$ = 0.58, $\alpha$ = 0.115, $\beta$ = 0.147; optimal granularity rises 8 → 64 from 3e18 to 6e24 FLOPs; expert = FF width "almost never optimal". → H4
- Dai et al. 2024 — DeepSeekMoE (arXiv:2401.06066). Fine-grained + shared experts: 2B matches GShard-2.9B (1.5× expert params and FLOPs); 16B matches LLaMA2-7B at ≈ 40 % FLOPs; 145B matches DeepSeek-67B at 28.5 % (18.2 %). → H4, H6
- DeepSeek-AI 2024 — DeepSeek-V3 (arXiv:2412.19437). 671B / 37B active; 256 routed + 1 shared per layer, top-8, sigmoid aux-loss-free balance, MTP; 14.8T tokens in 2.788M H800-h with no loss spike or rollback. → H4, H11, H13
- Clark et al. 2022 — Unified Scaling Laws for Routed Language Models (arXiv:2202.01169). Routing gain vanishes at $N_{cutoff}$ ≈ 937B (S-BASE), 85B (RL-R), 83B (hash); recommend $E$ ∈ {64, 128}, larger $E$ "diminishing returns" at 130B tokens. → H4, H6
- Gale et al. 2022 — MegaBlocks (arXiv:2211.15841; MLSys 2023). Block-sparse dropless MoE: up to 40 % faster than Tutel, 2.4× over Megatron dense; no token dropping, so dropless is the training baseline. → H4, H11
- Fedus et al. 2021 — Switch Transformer (arXiv:2101.03961; JMLR 2022): top-1 routing, up to 7× pretraining speedup vs T5-Base at equal FLOPs. Lepikhin et al. 2020 — GShard (arXiv:2006.16668; ICLR 2021): 600B, 2048 experts, top-2, 2048 TPUv3 for 4 days. → H4, H10
- Ludziejewski et al. 2025 — Joint MoE Scaling Laws (arXiv:2502.05172; ICML 2025). $L = a\bar E^{\delta} N_{act}^{\alpha+\gamma\ln\bar E} + b\bar E^{\omega} D^{\beta+\zeta\ln\bar E} + c$ (280 runs to 2.7B active); $E$ ≤ 8 beats compute-optimal dense at equal total params if trained on $E$× tokens; inference FLOPs −36 % ($E$ = 2), −61 % ($E$ = 4). → H4, H6, H10
- Tian et al. 2025 — Towards Greater Leverage (arXiv:2507.17702). 300+ models to 28B: efficiency leverage is a power law in activation ratio; granularity is U-shaped with optimum ≈ 8–12; one shared expert optimal; Ling-mini-beta 0.85B active = 6.1B dense (7×) at 1T tokens. → H4, H6
- Zhao et al. 2025 — Towards a Comprehensive Scaling Law of MoE (arXiv:2509.23678). 446 runs, 133M–3.4B, ≤ 256 experts: optimal active experts $G$ ≈ 7 and shared ratio 13–31 % (Δloss < 0.001) independent of $N$, $D$; optimal $N_a/N$ falls 40 % → 22 % from 30B → 671B. → H4, H6
- Li et al. 2026 — Slicing and Dicing: Configuring Optimal MoEs (arXiv:2605.11689). 2000+ runs to 6.6B: optimal expert size depends only on active params, not total; shared experts, heterogeneous sizes and balancing method are second-order vs expert count and granularity; dropless always helps. → H4, H6
- Kimi Team 2025 — Kimi K2 (arXiv:2507.20534). 1T / 32B active, 384 routed + 1 shared, top-8 (sparsity 48): at validation loss 1.5, sparsity 48 needs 1.69× / 1.39× / 1.15× fewer FLOPs than sparsity 8 / 16 / 32. → H4, H6, H10
- Ling Team 2025 — Ling 2.0 (arXiv:2510.22115). 256 routed, top-8 + 1 shared (≈ 3.5 % activation) held fixed from 16B to 1T; aux-loss-free with zero-centred bias and gate scale 2.5; 7× active-compute leverage over dense. → H4, H11
- Muennighoff et al. 2024 (rev. 2025) — OLMoE (arXiv:2409.02060). 7B / 1B active, 64 experts top-8. Ablations at fixed active/total params: 8 → 32 experts ≈ +10 % HellaSwag/MMLU at 130B tokens, 32 → 64 +1–2 %; shared expert slightly worse; expert-choice 20 % faster but token-choice better. → H4, H6, H11
- Qiu et al. 2025 — Demons in the Detail (arXiv:2501.11873). Global-batch balancing loss (to 42.8B / 400B tokens) lowers perplexity and yields domain-level specialisation vs micro-batch LBL; Qwen3 (Yang et al. 2025, arXiv:2505.09388) adopts it with 128 experts, top-8, no shared expert. → H4, H11
- Boix-Adsera & Rigollet 2025 — The power of fine-grained experts (arXiv:2505.06839). Theory: exponential separation in expressivity with granularity (more, smaller active experts) at fixed active parameters. → H4
- Han & Zhong 2025 (rev. 2026) — A Theoretical Framework for Aux-Loss-Free Load Balancing (arXiv:2512.03915). Bias update is a primal–dual assignment step: logarithmic expected regret under a step-size rule; monotone Lagrangian improvement in the deterministic case. → H11
- Hu et al. 2025 — Sigma-MoE-Tiny (arXiv:2512.16248). 20B / 0.5B active, 96 experts, top-1: loss-free balancing becomes ineffective in lower layers at extreme sparsity; a progressive sparsification schedule restores balance. → H11
- Sun et al. 2026 — Expert Threshold Routing (arXiv:2603.11535). Causal expert-choice via per-expert EMA thresholds: 2.4B on FineWeb-Edu, −0.067 CE vs token-choice, same loss at 1.6× fewer tokens, balanced without an auxiliary loss. → H11, H7
- Vankadara et al. 2026 — How to Scale MoE: from muP to MSSP (arXiv:2605.14200). Parameterisation that keeps learning-rate transfer and monotone improvement when width, expert count and top-k co-scale — use when sweeping $N_e$ at fixed budget. → H4, H6

## P5 / P9 — Fabric, placement, collectives
- NVIDIA 2025 — *DGX SuperPOD Reference Architecture featuring DGX GB200*, RA-11338-001 (rev. 2025-09-03, page updated 2025-11-19; https://docs.nvidia.com/dgx-superpod/reference-architecture-scalable-infrastructure-gb200/latest/). NVL72 domain = 72 GPUs, 18 NVLink5 links per GPU (1.8 TB/s); SU = 8 racks = 576 GPUs; rail-aligned leaf–spine where each rail is one hop inside an SU. Replaces the vague "DGX SuperPOD reference architecture" entry; the H100 predecessor is RA-11333-001. → H5, scenario `gpu_today`.
- Graham et al. 2020 — SHARP Streaming-Aggregation Hardware Design and Evaluation (ISC High Performance 2020, LNCS; PMC7295336). In-switch reduction on HDR reaches ≈ 95 % of link bandwidth, 2–5× the reduction bandwidth of host-based all-reduce for medium/large messages (≈ 5× at 128 KB); PyTorch end-to-end +18 %. Replaces "Mellanox/NVIDIA SHARP". → H9.
- Wang, Ghobadi et al. 2023/2025 — Rail-only: A Low-Cost High-Performance Network for Training LLMs with Trillion Parameters (arXiv:2307.12169; IEEE Micro 45(2) 2025 as "Spine-Free Networks for LLM Training"). Dropping the spine keeps training throughput while cutting network cost 38–77 % and power 37–75 %; MoE all-to-all pays 8.2–11.2 % completion-time overhead. → H5 (the ≤ 5 % loss target sits below this measured overhead), H1.
- Zhao et al. 2025 — Insights into DeepSeek-V3: Scaling Challenges and Reflections on Hardware for AI Architectures (arXiv:2505.09343, ISCA '25). Eight-plane two-layer fat tree: 16 384 GPUs at $4.39 k/endpoint vs $7.5 k for a three-layer tree; node-limited routing (≤ 4 of 8 nodes per token) cuts deduplicated IB bytes from 8t to ≤ 4t; asks for hardware packet replication for dispatch. → H5, H9.
- DeepSeek-AI (Zhao et al.) 2025 — DeepEP: expert-parallel all-to-all library (github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepEP; no arXiv — cite via 2505.09343). V2 README (2026): dispatch/combine 90/81 GB/s RDMA per GPU at EP 16 on CX7, 726/740 GB/s over NVLink at EP 8 (SM100); > 40 GB/s per GPU on 128 H800 in the ISCA paper. → H5, H9 (per-port budget input to S3).
- DeepSeek-AI 2025 — EPLB: Expert Parallelism Load Balancer (github.com/deepseek-ai/EPLB; hierarchical replication + group-to-node packing from the DeepSeek-V3 report). Measured effect (SGLang team, lmsys.org 2025-05-05, 96 H100): EPLB gives 1.49× prefill and 2.54× decode throughput vs unbalanced EP with 32 redundant experts (288 slots); EP+PD reaches 5× vanilla TP16. → H9, H11.
- Ultra Ethernet Consortium 2025/2026 — *Ultra Ethernet Specification v1.0* (2025-06-11) and v1.0.2 (2026-01-28; https://ultraethernet.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/01/UE-Specification-1.0.2-1.pdf). v1.0 standardises optional switch-offloaded In-Network Collectives (AllReduce etc.) over Ethernet (UEC blog 2024-08-29); INC is one of the consortium's three 2026 work items (Network World, 2026-01-07), i.e. no shipping implementations or public numbers yet; multicast for dispatch is not specified. Replaces the "(verify)" entry. → H9 (treat INC on Ethernet as a scenario switch, not a given).
- Zhang et al. 2026 — TAOT: Topology-Aware Optimal Transport for Dynamic Expert Replica Placement in MoE Training (arXiv:2608.03676). Sinkhorn placement of replicas against a topology cost matrix: weighted expert-communication cost down up to 74 % (≈ 3.8×), 1.43× end-to-end training speedup. → H9 (placement-only prior for the 3× threshold).
- Han et al. 2025/2026 — GRACE-MoE: Grouping and Replication with Locality-Aware Routing for Efficient Distributed MoE Inference (arXiv:2509.25041). Co-activation-based non-uniform grouping + load-skew replication + locality-aware replica choice: up to 4.66× speedup over existing multi-node EP serving; abstract gives no bytes number — read §Eval for the traffic split. → H9.
- Liu et al. 2026 — Director: Accelerating Distributed MoE Serving via Online Proactive Expert Placement (arXiv:2607.08782). Prediction-driven online placement with (1+ε) relaxation and near-zero-downtime migration: end-to-end latency −11…55 % on Mistral/DeepSeek/Qwen MoEs. → H9, H11.
- Cohen (Ori) et al. 2026 — MoX: Efficient MoE Routing on Direct-Connect Topologies (arXiv:2607.20220). Token-aware multicast trees on static optical/direct-connect fabrics: dispatch bottleneck-link load −47 % on a 1 024-TPU Boardfly model, full MoE block up to 1.8× faster than min-hop (ASTRA-sim, recorded traces). → H9 (multicast-only prior ≈ 1.9×).
- Zhang (Qijun) et al. 2026 — Accelerating MoE with Dynamic In-Switch Computing on Multi-GPUs, "DySHARP" (arXiv:2605.05607). Extends SHARP with dynamic multimem addressing so dispatch multicast and combine reduction run in the switch: up to 1.79× over DeepEP-style EP. → H9 (closest existing realisation of the note's P9).
- Xu et al. 2026 — Exploiting Multicast for Accelerating Collective Communication, "MultiWrite" (arXiv:2605.22428). Multicast AllGather/AlltoAll-dispatch in production on Ascend NPUs: operator latency −33 % by removing duplicate packets. → H9.
- Cohen (Evyatar) et al. 2026 — Incast-Free MoE Rate-Based Scheduling (arXiv:2607.26340). Shows round-robin EP dispatch causes exponential incast; a NIC-implementable proactive rate scheduler holds ≈ 100 % link utilisation in simulation (no absolute numbers in abstract). → H5, H11 (scheduler model for S1/S3).
- Mao et al. 2025/2026 — UCCL-EP: Portable Expert-Parallel Communication (arXiv:2512.19849). CPU-proxied GPUDirect RDMA matches DeepEP on NVIDIA/CX7 and gives up to 2.1× dispatch/combine throughput on EFA; +45 % DeepSeek-V3 training throughput on 16 AMD+Broadcom nodes. → H5 (portability of the ≈ 40–90 GB/s per-GPU budget beyond ConnectX).
- Yan et al. 2026 — Scalable Training of Mixture-of-Experts Models with Megatron Core (arXiv:2603.07685, NVIDIA). Production MoE training stack: 1 233 TFLOPS/GPU (GB300) and 1 048 (GB200) on DeepSeek-V3-685B; reference for what "fine-grained MoE at scale" costs today. → H1 baseline, H5.

## P6 / P7 — Repeated block, adaptive depth
Verified 2026-09-02 against arXiv / OpenReview / Semantic Scholar. Percentages are relative perplexity unless "nats" is stated; "param-matched" = same total parameters, "depth-matched" = same unrolled depth and FLOPs but fewer parameters.

- Dehghani et al. 2019 — Universal Transformers (arXiv:1807.03819, ICLR 2019). Shared block with ACT: LAMBADA LM ppl 142 → 131 (6 fixed steps) → 130 (ACT, mean 8.2 ± 2.1 steps); +0.9 BLEU WMT14 En-De; no param-matched LM baseline. → H6, H7
- Lan et al. 2020 — ALBERT (arXiv:1909.11942, ICLR 2020). All-layer sharing at BERT-base costs −2.5 avg points (82.3 → 79.8, 108M → 31M params, E = 768), −1.5 at E = 128; sharing attention alone costs −0.7, FFN sharing carries the loss. → H6 (naive sharing is not free)
- Csordás et al. 2024 — MoEUT: Mixture-of-Experts Universal Transformers (arXiv:2405.16039, NeurIPS 2024). Param-matched shared-layer MoE (FF + attention experts, layer groups G = 2, peri-layernorm) beats dense on C4: 18.97 → 18.30 ppl at 44M, 11.15 → 10.90 at 1B, fewer MACs; G = 1 (fully shared) is worse than G = 2. → H6
- Bae et al. 2024 — Relaxed Recursive Transformers: Effective Parameter Sharing with Layer-wise LoRA (arXiv:2410.20672, ICLR 2025). Gemma 2B looped ×2 (1B): 51.7 % avg few-shot after 15B uptrain tokens vs 61.7 % full; depth-wise LoRA rank 512 + 60B tokens recovers 58.4 % vs 58.6 % for the equally uptrained full model; early exit + depth-wise batching up to 2.66× throughput. → H6, H7 (L5b)
- Geiping et al. 2025 — Scaling up Test-Time Compute with Latent Reasoning: A Recurrent Depth Approach (arXiv:2502.05171, NeurIPS 2025). 3.5B (2 prelude / 4-layer core / 2 coda, sandwich norm, input injected every step, random initial state, log-normal-Poisson r̄ = 32, truncated backprop k = 8), 800B tokens; ARC-C 24.1 → 38.2 and HellaSwag 29.3 → 65.2 from r = 1 → 32; zero-shot KL-threshold exit (5e-4) leaves MTBench 5.63 → 5.56. No learned halting. → H7, stability
- Wang et al. 2025 — Hierarchical Reasoning Model (arXiv:2506.21734). 27M, two coupled recurrent modules, Q-learned ACT halt, one-step gradient (no BPTT); ARC-AGI-1 40.3 % from 1 000 examples. ARC Prize re-run: 32 % semi-private, and a same-size plain transformer lands within ~5 pp — the outer refinement loop, not the hierarchy, carries it; TRM (Jolicoeur-Martineau 2025, arXiv:2510.04871) gets 45 % with a 7M two-layer net. The DFM Mimir line builds on it via HRM-Text (both below). → H7 (puzzle tasks only; not LM loss)
- Graves 2016 — Adaptive Computation Time for Recurrent Neural Networks (arXiv:1603.08983). Halting probability + ponder cost + remainder step; on Hutter-Prize Wikipedia char-LM it moves compute to word/sentence boundaries with no significant bpc gain — the null prior for H7; large gains only on synthetic parity/addition/sorting. → H7
- Raposo et al. 2024 — Mixture-of-Depths (arXiv:2404.02258). Per-block top-k token routing (12.5 % capacity every other block): equal training loss with up to 50 % fewer forward FLOPs/token than the isoFLOP-optimal dense model, or ≈ 1.5 % better log-prob at equal training FLOPs; per-block skipping, not sequential halting. → H7
- Schuster et al. 2022 — CALM: Confident Adaptive Language Modeling (arXiv:2207.07061, NeurIPS 2022). ## P15 — Indexed / sparse attention, KV offload
Verified 2026-09-02 against arXiv / OpenReview / DeepSeek GitHub. Bears on H15a (global attention over final vectors only, ≤ 2 %), H15b (shared block indexer over $\mathcal{G}$, ≤ 1 % long-context; cold hits rare), H16 (`note64` fit). Thresholds: ADR-013; attention semantics: ADR-021, `01 §4.3/§6`, `02 §7`.

### Retrieval-augmented attention (2022–2023)
- Wu et al. 2022 — Memorizing Transformers (ICLR 2022, arXiv:2203.08913). kNN (k = 32) over a non-differentiable K/V memory in one layer: PG-19 ppl 12.34 (XL cache) → 12.29 (8 k mem) → 11.62 (65 k); arXiv-math 2.67 → 2.31; a 200 M model + 8 k memory matches a 1 B vanilla model. - Bertsch et al. 2023 — Unlimiformer (NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2305.01625). One FAISS index shared by all heads and decoder layers, top-k keys per head; BART-base GovReport R-1 48.7 → 49.7 training-free, 56.6 trained; 500 k-token BookSum; 2 GB index per 1 M tokens (fp16, d = 1024); 4.5× GPU time at 143 k. → H15b (single index per sequence shared across heads/layers is a working precedent).

Rank-160 keys on GPU, values on host, 8-token landmark chunks, 1.56 % sparse budget: RULER-128 k 86.9 vs 86.7 full (Llama-3-8B-1M), 83.6 vs 85.5 (Llama-3.1-8B); 6× batch, 3.04× throughput on A100. → H15b, H16 (≈ 1.5 % of KV resident is enough for ± 2 RULER points).

### Trainable sparse / indexed attention (2025–2026)
- Yuan et al. 2025 — Native Sparse Attention (ACL 2025, arXiv:2502.11089). 27B/3B-active MoE, 270 B tokens; compress 32, select 16 × 64-token blocks, window 512, one selector per GQA group: LongBench 0.469 vs 0.437 full, general 0.456 vs 0.443, perfect 64 k needle; 9.0× fwd / 6.0× bwd / 11.6× decode at 64 k. → H15b (trained block selection loses nothing; supports ≤ 1 %).
- Lu et al. 2025 — MoBA (Moonshot, arXiv:2502.13189). Block = 512–4 096 tokens, top-3 blocks (81–95 % sparsity); 568 M–2.1 B scaling: validation-loss gap vs full ≤ 1e-3 at 8 k, marginally larger on trailing tokens at 32 k, shrinking with size; Llama-8B-1M: RULER-128 k 0.782 vs 0.785, LongBench 0.483 vs 0.482; 6.5× at 1 M. → H15b (the smallest-scale published loss delta for block attention: ≈ 0.03 % at 8 k).
- Nawrot et al. 2025 — The Sparse Frontier (arXiv:2504.17768). Six sparse methods, nine tasks, up to 128 k and 95 % sparsity: larger-sparser beats smaller-dense at equal cost; longer sequences tolerate more sparsity; but no single budget is safe for every task. → H15b (motivates a variable $K_{idx}$; a fixed budget will fail some RULER sub-task).
- DeepSeek-AI 2025 — DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp tech report (GitHub PDF, no arXiv) and DeepSeek-V3.2 (arXiv:2512.02556). DSA lightning indexer: 64 heads × d 128, ReLU, FP8, one score per (query, key) shared by all 128 query heads under MLA-MQA; top-k = 2 048 tokens; trained by KL to head-summed dense attention (2.1 B tokens warm-up, lr 1e-3, backbone frozen) then 943.7 B sparse tokens; V3.2-Exp vs V3.1-Terminus: MMLU-Pro 85.0/85.0, GPQA 79.9/80.7, AIME-25 89.3/88.4, AA-LCR +4, BrowseComp 40.1/38.5; indexer still O(L²) but far cheaper than MLA; > 50 % long-context API price cut. → H15b (head-shared indexer + KL-to-teacher is exactly `01 §6.2–6.3`; cost prior for $d_{idx}$).
- Gao et al. 2026 — HySparse (arXiv:2602.03560). Full-attention layers act as the *oracle* selector and their KV is reused by the sparse layers: 80B MoE with 5 of 49 layers full, ≈ 10× KV reduction at matched quality. → H15a, H15b (KV sharing + selection from one representation, closest to "one global cache, many readers").
- Xu et al. 2026 — HISA (arXiv:2603.28458); Song et al. 2026 — MISA (arXiv:2605.07363). Training-free fixes for the DSA indexer's O(L²) scan: block-then-token hierarchy (HISA) or routing to 8 of 64 indexer heads (MISA) match DSA on LongBench within 0.5 pt with 4–8× less indexer work. → H15b (block-level pre-filter over token indexer costs < 0.5 pt; our $\kappa_{blk}$ design is the HISA first stage).
- Sun et al. 2026 — You Only Index Once / CLSA (arXiv:2606.06467). YOCO-based 4B, 32 layers; one single-head indexer shared by all 16 cross-decoder layers; 2 048 of 32 k tokens (1:16) captures 80 % of dense attention mass; CE-loss gap ≤ 0.006 nats across Books/arXiv/code; best RULER-32 k average; 7.6× decode, 17.1× end-to-end at 128 k on B200. → H15a, H15b (the direct precedent for one indexer over one shared cache; gap ≈ 0.2 % of loss).
- Lai et al. 2026 — MiniMax Sparse Attention (arXiv:2606.13392). 109B/6B-active trained from scratch, 3 T tokens; 128-token blocks, k = 16 (2 048 budget), one index query head per GQA group and one shared index key head, 40 B-token indexer warm-up before sparse training; HELMET-128 k −0.60, RULER-128 k +0.12 vs GQA; 28.4× less attention compute at 1 M. → H15b (from-scratch block-sparse at scale: ≈ ± 0.5 pt; warm-up = 1.3 % of tokens sets our L9 curriculum prior).
- Wang et al. 2026 — FlashMemory-DeepSeek-V4 (arXiv:2606.09079; Tencent/HKUST-GZ/Tsinghua, not DeepSeek). Lookahead indexer trained *without* the backbone (1 H20 GPU-hour, dual-encoder on cached hidden states): keeps 13.5 % of KV on GPU; LongBench-v2 70.0 vs 68.1, RULER-512 k 89.6 vs 88.3; 2.8× throughput on 8×H20. → H15b (13.5 % resident is a hit-rate prior for hot+warm; indexer can be trained off-line from a dense run).

### Cross-iteration / cross-layer KV sharing (H15a, H16)
- Brandon et al. 2024 — Cross-Layer Attention (arXiv:2405.12981). Share K/V between adjacent layers: 1B/30 B tokens MQA ppl 13.54 → 13.60 with CLA2 (KV/token 10 240 → 5 120 B, +0.4 %); CLA4 13.95 (+3 %); 3B/100 B: 9.52 → 9.34. → H15a, H16 (2× sharing is ≈ free; 4× costs ≈ 3 % — bounds how far per-iteration caches can be collapsed).
- Sun et al. 2024 — You Only Cache Once (NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2405.05254). Self-decoder produces one global KV, cross-decoder reads it: YOCO-3B (1.6 T tokens) LM-eval avg 0.636, matches Transformer scaling 160 M–13 B; KV memory 9× lower at 1 M (3B), ≈ 80× at 65B; prefill 512 k 180 s → < 6 s; 0.98 single-needle at 128 k. → H15a (a single cache built from one representation per token costs nothing measurable at 3B — the strongest prior that 2 % is loose).
- O'Neill & Reid 2026 — Looped Latent Attention (arXiv:2607.15456). In weight-tied looped models the per-loop K/V of a token lies on a short low-rank trajectory: post-training codec gives 21.3× cache compression on Ouro-1.4B (batch 32 → 768 on H200), near-lossless to 32× on Huginn-3.5B, beats cross-layer sharing and final-loop reuse. → H16, H15a (per-iteration caches in $W$ are compressible ≈ 20×; final-vector-only global cache is the limit case).

### KV offload to host / NVMe (H15b-S, H16)
- Sheng et al. 2023 — FlexGen (ICML 2023, arXiv:2303.06865). LP-scheduled weight/KV/activation placement over GPU/CPU/disk, 4-bit KV: OPT-175B at 1 token/s on a 16 GB T4, 100× throughput over prior offloading. → H16 (throughput-mode offload baseline; latency-blind).
- Lee et al. 2024 — InfiniGen (OSDI 2024, arXiv:2406.19707). Whole KV on host; rehearses next layer's attention with 30 % of query columns to prefetch < 10 % of KV (cap 20 %); up to 3.0× over FlexGen/H2O on A6000 + PCIe 3.0 with ppl ≈ full-cache. → H15b (speculated selection keeps < 10 % hot with no ppl loss; sets the warm-tier fraction).
- Ren et al. 2025 — I/O characterisation of model/KV offload to NVMe (CHEOPS ’25, ACM). DeepSpeed/FlexGen traces: KV-offload I/O is 128 KiB-dominated, read 2.0 GiB/s vs write 11 MiB/s, and offloading does not saturate the SSD. → H15b-S (matches our `local_3060` finding: ≥ 64 KiB fetches, ≥ 8 in flight, or the drive idles).
- Xu et al. 2025 — NOSA (arXiv:2510.13602; 1B/3B/8B). NSA-style trainable sparsity with an explicit locality constraint (k = 4 096, k_q = 1 024 at 16 k): ≈ 2× fewer cache misses than InfLLMv2 under offload; 5.04× decode throughput vs full at 96 k, 1.83× vs ShadowKV; keeps HELMET/LongBench; NOSI reaches 83 % of PCIe peak. → H15b (train the selector for temporal locality — the cold-hit rate is a *trainable* quantity, not just measured).
- Qiu et al. 2026 — Tutti (arXiv:2605.03375). GPU-native io_uring path HBM ↔ NVMe without CPU: SSD-backed KV ≈ DRAM-backed at 75 % hit rate with 29 GB/s SSD read; TTFT −78 % vs LMCache+GDS, 2× request rate, −27 % cost. → H15b-S (production SSD tier assumes ≈ 75 % hit and tens of GB/s; our single SN850X gives 7.3 GB/s at ≥ 256 KiB).
- Bocharnikov et al. 2026 — KV Cache Offloading for Context-Intensive Tasks (arXiv:2604.08426). Low-rank key projection and landmark-based offload (ShadowKV-style) degrade sharply on extraction-heavy Text2JSON on Llama-3/Qwen-3. → H15b (needle/RULER alone will not catch selector failure; keep a copy/extraction task in the L9 suite).

### Long-context evaluation
- Hsieh et al. 2024 — RULER (COLM 2024, arXiv:2404.06654). 13 synthetic tasks (NIAH variants, multi-hop tracing, aggregation), length-controllable; of 17 models only half hold up at 32 k. → H15b (the 8–32 k subset ADR-014 names; multi-key NIAH and aggregation are the sub-tasks sparse selectors fail).
- Yen et al. 2025 — HELMET (ICLR 2025, arXiv:2410.02694). 7 application categories to 128 k, 59 models: NIAH does not predict downstream; categories correlate weakly. → H15b (needs instruction-following; at ~100 M only the RAG/recall categories are meaningful — do not gate on HELMET).
- Bai et al. 2025 — LongBench v2 (ACL 2025, arXiv:2412.15204). 503 multiple-choice questions, 8 k–2 M words; humans 53.7 %, o1-preview 57.7 %. → H15b (out of reach at `small`; listed so results.md says why it is omitted).

### ANN indexes for the cold tier
- Malkov & Yashunin 2018 — HNSW (IEEE TPAMI 42(4), arXiv:1603.09320). Multi-layer navigable small-world graph, logarithmic search; the CPU default. → H15b-S.
- Jégou, Douze & Schmid 2011 — Product Quantization (TPAMI 33(1)); Johnson, Douze & Jégou 2019 — Billion-scale similarity search with GPUs (IEEE Trans. Big Data, arXiv:1702.08734); Douze et al. 2024 — The Faiss library (arXiv:2401.08281). IVF-PQ is the 2011 paper; 2019 is the GPU implementation; 2024 the library overview. Our `experiments/007`: IVF4000,PQ96x8 at 1 M × 768-d, 7.2 k QPS batched (nprobe 8), 1.35 ms single query, 53 s build on 12 cores. → H15b-S.
- Ootomo et al. 2024 — CAGRA (ICDE 2024, arXiv:2308.15136). GPU graph index: 33–77× HNSW throughput at large batch and 3.4–53× single-query at 95 % recall; build 2.2–27× faster; now `faiss` `GpuIndexCagra` via cuVS. → H15b-S (the cold-tier index can live on the GPU at 1–10 M vectors; RetrievalAttention shows IVF recall is the weak point).
- Liu et al. 2026 — GPU graph vector search: taxonomy and study (arXiv:2602.16719). Eight datasets: distance computation dominates on-GPU; host↔GPU transfer dominates real latency at scale. → H15b-S (the fetch, not the search, is the cold-tier bottleneck — consistent with `02 §7`'s bytes-per-hit formula).

## P17 — Memory and retrieval
- Lample et al. 2019 — Large Memory Layers with Product Keys (arXiv:1907.05242, NeurIPS 2019). Up to 1 B memory parameters at negligible FLOPs; a 12-layer + PKM model beats a 24-layer transformer at 2× inference speed. → H17 (the L8 mechanism).
- He 2024 — Mixture of A Million Experts, PEER (arXiv:2407.04153, Google DeepMind). Product-key retrieval over > 10⁶ single-neuron experts: isoFLOP C4 perplexity 20.63 vs 21.41 MoE and 21.92 PKM at 6e18 FLOPs. → H17, H4.
- Berges et al. 2024/2025 — Memory Layers at Scale (arXiv:2412.09764, **ICML 2025**, PMLR v267 — not ICLR). At 1.3 B base + ≈ 1 M keys: NaturalQuestions 7.76 → 13.68 %, TriviaQA 32.64 → 42.89 F1 vs dense; at 8 B/1 T tokens NQ +1.8, TQA +4.5; gains appear from ≈ 1 M keys, scale to 64 M keys (128 B params); Memory+ beats MoE and PEER at matched compute+params on factual QA. → H17 (sets the knowledge-eval prior and the ≥ 1 M-entry table size).
- Huang et al. 2024/2025 — Ultra-Sparse Memory Network, UltraMem (arXiv:2411.12364, ICLR 2025, ByteDance). Memory layer with 20 M slots matches MoE quality at equal params/activation with 2–6× faster inference at common batch sizes. → H17, H1.
- Huang et al. 2025 — UltraMemV2: Memory Networks Scaling to 120B Parameters with Superior Long-Context Learning (arXiv:2508.18756). 120 B total / 2.5 B active matches an 8-expert MoE at iso-compute+params; +1.6 long-context memorisation, +6.2 multi-round memorisation, +7.9 in-context learning; activation density matters more than sparse-param count. → H17.
- Cheng (Xin) et al. 2026 — Conditional Memory via Scalable Lookup: A New Axis of Sparsity for LLMs, "Engram" (arXiv:2601.07372, DeepSeek). O(1) n-gram-hashed memory scaled to 27 B params beats an iso-param, iso-FLOPs MoE: MMLU +3.4, CMMLU +4.0, BBH +5.0, ARC-C +3.7, HumanEval +3.0, MATH +2.4; MQ-NIAH 84.2 → 97.0; U-shaped memory-vs-MoE allocation law. → H17 (ARC-C +3.7 is the direct prior for ADR-013's ≥ 1-point test), H6.
- Lin et al. 2025 — Continual Learning via Sparse Memory Finetuning (arXiv:2510.15103, Meta). Updating only highly activated memory slots: NQ F1 drop after new-fact learning 11 % vs 89 % (full FT) and 71 % (LoRA). → H17 (table refresh without forgetting), H18.
- Zhao (Tianyu) et al. 2026 — Fast-weight Product Key Memory, FwPKM (arXiv:2601.00671). PKM slots updated by chunk-level gradient steps at train and test time: trained at 4 k, NIAH generalises to 128 k with fixed per-token cost. → H17, H15b.
- Cheng (Runxi) et al. 2026 — Memory Grafting: Scaling LM Pre-training via Offline Conditional Memory (arXiv:2605.20948). Frozen hidden states of a donor model stored offline and fetched by O(1) longest-suffix match: avg benchmark 53.86 vs 51.95 (MoE) and 52.43 (Engram) at 2.8 B. → H17 (offline-precomputed memory is the L8b pattern).
- Jie et al. 2025 — Mixture of Lookup Experts, MoLE (arXiv:2503.15798, ICML 2025). Experts re-parameterised as input-id lookup tables kept off-GPU: dense-model inference speed at MoE quality, negligible transfer. → H17, H16 (memory-tier placement of the table).
- Borgeaud et al. 2022 — RETRO (arXiv:2112.04426, ICML 2022). Chunk-level (64-token) retrieval from a 2 T-token store with frozen BERT keys and precomputed neighbours; matches GPT-3/Jurassic-1 on the Pile with 25× fewer params. → H17 (L8b template).
- Wang et al. 2023/2024 — InstructRetro (arXiv:2310.07713, ICML 2024, NVIDIA). Retro 48 B: continued pretraining of a 43 B GPT on 100 B tokens with retrieval from 1.2 T tokens costs +2.58 % GPU-hours and lowers perplexity; the largest retrieval-pretrained LM. → H17 (cost prior for L8b).
- Shao et al. 2024 — Scaling Retrieval-Based Language Models with a Trillion-Token Datastore, MassiveDS (arXiv:2407.12854). 1.4 T-token open datastore; LM loss improves monotonically with store size and small model + large store beats larger model on knowledge tasks at fixed training compute. → H17 (corpus-slice size for L8b).
- Cao et al. 2025 — Memory Decoder: A Pretrained, Plug-and-Play Memory for LLMs (arXiv:2508.09874). Small decoder distilled from a kNN-LM retriever; plugs into any Qwen/Llama with the same tokenizer: −6.17 perplexity on biomed/finance/law. → H17 (parametric substitute for a datastore, cf. ADR-009).
- Khandelwal et al. 2020 — kNN-LM (arXiv:1911.00172, ICLR 2020). Interpolating with a nearest-neighbour datastore: WikiText-103 perplexity 18.65 → 15.79 with no training. → H17.

## P18 — Multi-tenant adapters
- Sheng et al. 2023/2024 — S-LoRA: Serving Thousands of Concurrent LoRA Adapters (arXiv:2311.03285, MLSys 2024). Unified paging + custom kernels: up to 4× throughput vs PEFT/vLLM and "several orders of magnitude" more adapters per GPU (thousands). → H18-S.
- Chen et al. 2023 — Punica: Multi-Tenant LoRA Serving (arXiv:2310.18547, MLSys 2024). SGMV batched-gather kernel: 12× throughput vs per-adapter serving, +2 ms/token latency. → H18-S.
- Wu et al. 2024 — dLoRA: Dynamically Orchestrating Requests and Adapters for LoRA LLM Serving (OSDI '24). Merge/unmerge + request–adapter co-migration: up to 57.9× vs vLLM, 26.0× vs PEFT, 1.8× lower latency than S-LoRA. → H18-S.
- Brüel-Gabrielsson et al. 2024/2025 — Compress then Serve: Serving Thousands of LoRA Adapters with Little Overhead (arXiv:2407.00066). Joint-basis compression of 1 000 LoRAs keeps task quality and retains ≈ 80 % of single-LoRA throughput. → H18-S (the ≤ 5 % throughput target is below this 20 % loss).
- Iliakopoulou et al. 2024/2025 — Chameleon: Adaptive Caching and Scheduling for Many-Adapter LLM Inference (arXiv:2411.17741, MICRO '25). Adapter cache in idle GPU memory + adapter-aware scheduling on top of S-LoRA: 1.5× throughput, P99 TTFT −80.7 %. → H18-S.
- Jaiswal et al. 2025 — LoRAServe: Serving Heterogeneous LoRA Adapters in Distributed LLM Inference Systems (arXiv:2511.22880). Rank-aware placement/routing with GPUDirect RDMA: up to 2× throughput, 9× lower tail TTFT, 50 % fewer GPUs at SLO. → H18-S.
- Chen (Hongyu) et al. 2026 — InfiniLoRA: Disaggregated Multi-LoRA Serving for LLMs (arXiv:2604.07173). Shared LoRA server decoupled from base-model (incl. MoE) inference: 3.05× serviceable request rate under SLO, +54 % adapters meeting SLO. → H18-S (adapters on the shared block served apart from experts).
- Dettmers et al. 2023 — QLoRA (arXiv:2305.14314, NeurIPS 2023). NF4 + paged optimisers: 65 B fine-tuned on one 48 GB GPU at 16-bit quality; Guanaco 99.3 % of ChatGPT on Vicuna. → H18.
- Biderman et al. 2024 — LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less (arXiv:2405.09673, TMLR 2024). At r = 16–256 LoRA substantially underperforms full FT on code/math continued pretraining; full-FT updates have 10–100× the rank of typical LoRAs, but LoRA forgets less. → H18 (weakens the ≥ 80 % prior for large-data adaptation).
- Schulman et al. 2025 — LoRA Without Regret (Thinking Machines blog, 2025-09-29, https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/). LoRA on all layers (incl. MLP/MoE) matches full FT for SFT while the dataset fits its capacity, and matches full FT in RL at rank 1; ≈ ⅔ the FLOPs per pass. → H18 (supports rank ≤ 4 for post-training-size data).
